# Steer2Edit native reproduction contract

Aryaman Singh Dev · 11 November 2026 · pre-specified before any run

Derived from [the Steer2Edit deep study](STEER2EDIT_DEEP_STUDY_AND_CONTRACT.md); discrepancy tags
(D1-D10) refer to that document's source-to-code map. Paper v4 (arXiv 2602.09870), repository commit
`86382c2f44fdfda2bafe80faf09634e6d729562c`. This contract authorizes nothing by itself: the GPU
run, the gated weight download and any paid judging need the owner's separate approval.

It preserves the paper's native endpoint and adds nothing to it.

**Target.** One cell: **LLaMA-2-7B-Chat, Steer2Edit, Average Safety = 88.15** (Table 5), with its
components GCG 95.10 and ADV-LLM 81.20, cross-checked against Table 11's 88.1. Reported but not
pass/fail: Avg Utility 28.00 (CSQA 45.01, CodeMMLU 20.06, GSM8K 18.93).

**Why this cell.** It is the only safety number corroborated by two independent tables; its model
is a supported original checkpoint; its attacks are pre-released; and its best configuration is
recoverable from the README. The main-paper safety result is a figure (Fig. 3) with no readable
numbers, so it is not a reproducible target.

**Checkpoint.** `meta-llama/Llama-2-7b-chat-hf`, gated — requires the owner's accepted licence and
`HF_TOKEN`. We pin an explicit `revision` SHA at launch and record it; the paper pins none (D10).

**Native endpoint.** Refusal rate = the fraction of responses containing any of the 32
`REFUSAL_SIGNAL` substrings, averaged over the two attacks. Unmodified list, unmodified matching.

**Data.** Calibration: the released 100 MaliciousInstruct + 100 Alpaca probes, generated by our own
run of step 1 (originals unrecoverable, D6). Evaluation: AdvBench behaviours 0–99 under the
released GCG suffixes and the released ADV-LLM `llama2-7b` suffixes. Utility: the three
`DATASET_MAP` datasets at their stated splits.

**Settings selection.** Two arms, both reported. (a) *Published setting*: ρ_attn 0.18, ρ_mlp 0.55,
α 0.8 directly. (b) *Published procedure*: the Table 2 refined grid — ρ_attn ∈ [0.16, 0.24] step
0.02, ρ_mlp ∈ [0.35, 0.55] step 0.05, α ∈ [0.70, 0.90] step 0.05 = **125 configurations** — with the
20-prompt degeneracy screen, ranked by the attribute metric, top ten taken to utility. Arm (a) is
the primary comparison; arm (b) tests whether the procedure re-finds the published optimum.

**Workload.** One probe-generation pass (200 prompts × ≤1024 tokens); 125 edit constructions
(cheap, reusing cached means); 200 attack generations for arm (a), up to 125 × 200 for arm (b);
10 × 3 utility evaluations for the top ten. Each evaluated configuration materializes a ~13 GB
checkpoint copy (D8). Released scripts default to `tensor_parallel_size=4`.

**Comparison criteria and uncertainty.** Primary: our average safety within ±5 percentage points of
88.15 counts as reproduced at the configuration level, given that the published value carries no
interval and `n` is undefined. We additionally report an exact binomial interval on our own
100-behaviour denominators per attack — an addition, labelled as such, since the paper reports none.

**Historical settings that cannot be recovered.** The exact probe responses behind the published
directions (D6); the checkpoint revision (D10); the meaning of `n`; the coarse-search outcomes that
produced the refined ranges.

**Deviations, labelled in advance.** Probe responses regenerated; checkpoint revision pinned by us;
library versions as available rather than the pinned `torch==2.6.0 / transformers==4.55.2 /
vllm==0.8.5` unless those install cleanly; any reduction in `tensor_parallel_size`; any subsetting
of the 125-configuration grid for budget. Each is a deviation row, never a silent substitution.

**Explicitly out of scope here.** Appendix N (VLM) reproduction — no released code. Our
MM-SafetyBench VLM diagnosis is a *separate adaptation contract*, written only after this native
check returns a number.

# Part 2 — VLM adaptation contract: MM-SafetyBench four conditions

Written 11 November 2026 in response to the owner's question about evaluating across the four
MM-SafetyBench data categories. **This is a separate contract.** It is not an original-paper
reproduction, and it does not start until Part 1 returns a number.

## P2.1 Why this is the real research question

Steer2Edit is a **text-LLM safety method**. Its safety experiments use text jailbreaks (GCG,
ADV-LLM) on LLaMA-2-7B-Chat and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2. v4's Appendix N does apply it to
Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, but it edits only the **LLM backbone**, explicitly leaves the vision encoder
untouched, and in its own words evaluates "safety and utility ... on **text-only benchmarks**".
No image enters the published pipeline at any point: the directions are text-derived, and the
attacks are text suffixes.

So the method is for safety alignment, it can be applied to a VLM, and it currently **relies
entirely on text**. That gap is the opportunity rather than a problem: the authors have supplied the
text-only VLM baseline, and our completed MM-SafetyBench panel supplies exactly the image-carried
conditions they omit.

## P2.2 The four conditions and what each one isolates

MM-SafetyBench routes every question through four carriers. Used together they decompose *where* a
text-derived edit works:

| Condition | Carrier of the harmful intent | What a failure here would mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TEXT | text only, no image | Closest to the published setting. If the edit fails here, the adaptation is broken, not the modality claim. |
| SD | Stable-Diffusion image of the concept, benign-looking text | Intent is carried by image *semantics*. Text-derived directions may never activate. |
| TYPO | harmful phrase rendered as typography in the image | Intent is carried by image *text*, reachable only through OCR. Tests whether the edit survives the vision-to-language path. |
| SD_TYPO | both | Strongest condition; the published MM-SafetyBench attack. |

The decisive comparison is the **profile across conditions, not a single number**. A text-derived
Steer2Edit edit that lowers harmful assistance on TEXT while leaving SD_TYPO unchanged is a clean,
publishable demonstrated limitation — and it is the specific limitation that motivates
image-matched calibration.

## P2.3 Target, model and reuse of the archived baseline

**Model:** LLaVA-1.5-7B. It has a completed 6,720-case baseline, and Steer2Edit edits its Vicuna
backbone with the same `o_proj` / `down_proj` component structure. Qwen2.5-VL-3B is the transfer
test afterwards, not the first target.

**Archived-baseline reuse is conditional.** The archive supplies the *unedited* arm for free only if
the identities match exactly: same checkpoint and revision, same four-condition prompts, same
decoding parameters, same judge and version. If any differ, the unedited arm must be regenerated.
Record the comparison explicitly; do not assume reuse.

## P2.4 Measurements, each with its own denominator

Reported separately, never collapsed into one "safety" number:

- **Harmful-assistance ASR** (semantic judge) — the primary safety endpoint here.
- **Native keyword refusal** using the released 32-string `REFUSAL_SIGNAL`, unmodified, so the
  published endpoint is preserved alongside ours.
- **Appropriate refusal** on harmful requests, which is not the complement of ASR.
- **Benign over-refusal**, the Appendix L concern, on benign twins.
- **Image-dependent capability**: OCR read-back and image-grounded answer correctness. This is the
  control that distinguishes a genuine safety gain from a destroyed vision path.
- **Empty or truncated outputs**, counted explicitly. InstructBLIP produced 798 empties in the
  baseline; an empty completion alone cannot establish its cause.
- **Failed calls, filtered judgments, coverage**, plus editing cost, latency and memory.

## P2.5 Splits, controls and honesty constraints

**Grouping rule.** All four variants of one question go in the **same** split. The TEXT, SD, TYPO
and SD_TYPO versions of a question are the same item in four carriers, not four independent items;
splitting them leaks.

**Exploratory status.** Previously inspected MM-SafetyBench outcomes are **exploratory** for this
new-method decision. Re-splitting already-inspected results does not create confirmation data.
Confirmation needs untouched items after an overlap check.

**Controls, all at matched calibration volume and edit budget:**

1. Unedited LLaVA-1.5-7B (identity-matched, reused or regenerated).
2. Original-method control: the released text-only recipe, unchanged.
3. Our proposed change (text+image matched calibration), same data volume and budget.
4. Shuffled-pair control, to test whether pairing contributes beyond extra data.
5. Random-direction control at the same edit budget.
6. Component-removal control, to test the explanation rather than only the outcome.

**Attribution limit.** The four released conditions differ in wording as well as carrier, so a
TEXT-versus-SD_TYPO difference cannot by itself be attributed to image presence. Matched wording
controls are required before any such claim — the same finding our own earlier work established
about the benchmark's imperative wrapper.

**Cost.** Changed weights produce new answers, and new answers require **new judgments**. The
existing judging budget covered the completed baseline only. Six passes over 6,720 cases per
configuration is not affordable per grid point, so the four-condition evaluation runs at the single
selected configuration, with the grid search done against the native text endpoint first.

## P2.6 Platform feasibility

LLaVA-1.5-7B in 16-bit is ~14 GiB. Kaggle's 2×T4 (tensor-parallel 2) and any Ampere-or-newer cloud
GPU are comfortable; a single 16 GiB T4 is tight and historically needed 4-bit loading for this
model, which is itself a deviation to record. Only Ampere or newer preserves the released bfloat16;
T4 and P100 lack bf16 support and force float16.
